# Stamford Campus Atlas project context

This is an interactive computer graphics project for the Stamford University Bangladesh campus. The player explores campus exteriors, reaches the portal under the swimming pool, and descends through a procedural ocean before returning to the pool deck. The [README](README.md) covers launch and player controls. The [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) records completed work and verification.

## Runtime and source map

Serve the repository root over HTTP in a WebGL 2 browser. The two HTML entry points pin Three.js 0.170.0 and its addons through matching jsDelivr import maps. Source modules run directly in the browser and the scenes generate their geometry and textures at runtime.

## Layout and navigation contracts

- The initial view uses a diorama over the live canvas. After exploration starts, a checkpoint is selected, or the player returns to the pool, the compact briefing exposes the scene and provides a resume button when paused. When paused, an **Exit to start** button is visible beside the resume button, and an **Exit** action (`X`) is available in the bottom HUD. Selecting exit pauses exploration, resets camera teleport to the starting checkpoint, restores the initial briefing screen and hero artwork, and clears active flight state. Pressing `Escape` while already paused also exits back to the start page. The minimap and depth meter remain hidden while their values update. The bottom controls are available on pause, and the ocean return link is also available on its landing screen. Weather keys update scene preferences directly.
- Briefing controls ready contract: while the 3D scene initializes (`data-ready="false"`), the primary enter button is hidden and disabled (`.primary:disabled, [data-ready="false"] .briefing-buttons { display: none; }`), and the loading state (`#load-state`) displays the loading message ("Building the campus scene…") centered within `.briefing-controls` (with matching `min-height: 50px`). Once `runtime.ready()` executes, `data-ready="true"` is set, status text is cleared (`#load-state:empty { display: none; }`), and the primary button appears in its exact normal position without layout shift. On WebGL context loss, `data-ready` reverts to `"false"`, the button hides, and the context paused message displays until restored.
- Campus coordinates use metres. `+Y` is up, `+X` is schematic east, and `+Z` points toward the entry street. Ground is at `y = 0` and land eye height is `y = 1.7`. Distances and orientation are estimates based on the local numbered `CG_Sessional` photo walkthrough. Use the map's `photoNotes` for frame ranges and observed features.
- The gate-side platform measures 20 ft 2 in by 12 ft 9 in with a 22 in rise, from `IMG_3138.JPG` through `IMG_3140.JPG`. Its initial alignment uses the gate in the loaded layout and the view from inside the entrance. The slab and pots form one movable `gate-platform` element. The element is seeded into the loaded map so older browser saves keep their storage key and exports capture its placement.
- The gate-side planting bed sits between the gate and that platform, based on `6- rightside of campus gate 1.jpg` and the marked scene view. Its estimated 5.2 m by 2.6 m footprint has a curved painted curb and a short return toward the gate. Trees and pots move together under the editable `gate-planting-bed` record.
- The stock guard and guardian rooms share a frontage. The guard room's blue corrugated metal front eave is 10 ft 3 in (3.1242 m) above grade, as measured in `IMG_3142.JPG`. The photographs inform the lower gray guardian eave, roof overhangs, glazing, and guard shelter. The rooms retain separate editable footprints, and the open shelter has colliders at its solid obstructions.
- The varsity bus uses the 8 ft 11 in (2.7178 m) roofline from `IMG_3146.JPG` and the map's existing width and length. Its [crest texture](assets/stamford-bus-crest.webp) is extracted from the same photo, with linework repaired where the measurement label covered it. The stock bus parks beside the CSE footprint. Layout loading carries forward browser saves from the previous stock map and updates the old placements when their stock poses still match.
- The shared scene runtime enables the camera coordinate HUD in development. Both scenes display world-space metres to one decimal place in the compact exploration view, including while paused. The Vite production build leaves the readout hidden.
- `boundary.points` is an ordered list of `[x, z]` corners and drives the terrain, editor placement limits, minimap, and camera fit. The default points in [CAMPUS_MAP.json](CAMPUS_MAP.json) come from the ten outer corners of `stamford-shape-boundary.png`, including the west notch and the angled southeastern projection. A uniform scale of 0.155 m per source pixel fits the traced proportions around the existing campus layout. The PNG supplies the shape and the scene supplies the scale and entrance alignment. Layouts with rectangular bounds still use the legacy street geometry.
- Boundary walls are stored as discrete linear segments in `boundary.walls` (`[{ id, label, a: [x, z], b: [x, z] }]`). Slicing divides a segment at any point into subsegments; individual segments can be selected and deleted (for instance, carving an opening at the university gate). Both default and custom boundaries build their 3D walls and walking capsule collision from `boundary.walls`, keeping physical meshes, obstacle boundaries, and minimaps in lockstep.
- The placement deck groups stored items into categorical tabs (**Buildings**, **Trees**, **Facilities**, **Doors**, and an **All** overview) with dynamic badges. Identical duplicate buildings share a single card with a `×N` multiplier; edited or resized duplicates branch into distinct cards labeled with footprint dimensions and floor count.
- **Preview survey outline** registers the same shape to the saved gate. It checks the pool and building footprints using a uniform scale and allows up to three degrees of rotation around the gate for clearance. **Load empty Stamford campus** applies the stock survey shape and stores placed objects together in one history entry. Generating or resetting perimeter walls leaves an opening where the frontage aligns with the gate. Layout loading upgrades older stock boundaries and carries forward saved custom outlines and wall edits.
- The stock map uses the same survey transform for CSE (A Academic), Civil (B Academic), Official (A Admin), the front garden, and 47 tree symbols. Building dimensions follow the centerlines of the labeled outlines. Official's `westWing` ratios describe the short leg of its L-shaped footprint relative to the main block. The wing has its own rotated collision box and shares the building's transform and floor count. `garden.points` stores the outer edge of the survey curb in garden-local metres. The lawn and minimap use those points directly, and the 3D curb extends inward from that edge. Each `surveyTrees` row stores world X, Z, canopy radius, and an optional tree type. Stable IDs keep those trees editable. The other facilities and checkpoints are arranged around the surveyed footprints.
- Loading a save with the previous surveyed footprint upgrades the garden trace and the earlier default dimensions. The saved garden transform and any independently resized buildings retain their edits. Older oval gardens receive a uniformly scaled copy of the survey outline within their former footprint.
- The tour goes from the gate and garden through CSE and Admission, past the bus and library corridor, then into the central ground and pool. The lawn includes a marked court from the references. The submerged ring is fictional.
- Campus obstacles use world-space XZ bounding boxes in the form `{ minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ }`. Surveyed building blocks also check the player radius in their rotated local axes. Horizontal movement resolves each axis in small steps so the player can slide along walls. Editing updates colliders and a blocked checkpoint uses a nearby clear position. HUD zone detection uses the first matching rectangle in `areas`, so keep specific areas ahead of larger overlapping areas.
- The pool opening, stair geometry, water height, and camera descent need to line up. A submerged camera within 2.2 m of the ring goes to `underwater.html`. The `P` shortcut uses the same transition. The return ring sits at `(8, -2, -3.3)` and returns to `index.html?arrival=pool`, which selects checkpoint 4 if the pool is placed. Storing the pool also stores its portal and closes the terrain opening.
- Ocean movement clamps camera Y from `-120` to `0`. The HUD layer changes at 30 m and 70 m. The fog and light profiles interpolate across 0, 25, 70, and 120 m, so the atmosphere can transition before the layer label changes.
- Buildings store `width` and `depth` in metres, integer `floors`, and `yaw` in degrees. The factory keeps its floor and fixture dimensions, refits the façade, and rebuilds the colliders and instance batches when the shape changes. Legacy building `scale` multipliers convert to physical dimensions and a rounded floor count when loaded. Other site records still use scale multipliers.
- The Official Building and Academic wing share one map entry, scene root, and editor object. `rearFloorSetback` sets the Official block’s third-floor setback. `academicWing` stores dimensions relative to the main block and the proportions for the one-storey rear strip and three-storey end bay. The stock `rearLink` joins the surveyed placements, and matching older editor layouts join wall to wall. The Academic wing uses the parent’s floor heights and trim material. Its end wall crosses the ground-floor recess at the junction, and the Official block’s lower roof slab covers the exposed frontage. The combined footprint drives the editor and minimap. Section-specific roof heights determine flight clearance. Migration keeps custom doors on the Academic wing as doors on the combined building.
- The Academic wing’s two walls overlooking the one-storey roof use solid plaster. The façade builder keeps the trim bands and skips window meshes on those faces when the model rebuilds.
- The five-storey CSE roof edge follows the 60 ft 2 in measurement in `IMG_3145.JPG`. The [roof fascia texture](assets/cse-fascia.webp) is perspective-corrected from `7- CSE building or Main building view from garden.jpg`. The first row of terracotta lattice sits on a white beam over a recessed base. The band above that row uses the neighboring Academic Building's first-floor elevation. The square trim starts on the next row.
- The two exterior stair flights follow the marked layout and the photos `42- CSE building Stair beside auditorium.jpg` and `42- A more natural view of the auditorium room.jpg`. The ground flight reaches the Academic wing’s lower roof. A second flight rises along the pink wall to the upper level, offset slightly left of the frontage roof trim so its treads, stringer, railing, and post stay clear while its upper landing deck bridges flush to the roof coping. In the stock survey layout, the ground flight runs parallel to the rear wall to fit the passage. The stair landings use the modeled roof elevations, and the flights and collision bounds rebuild with the stepped wing.
- The CSE garden monument moves with the building. Its estimated dimensions come from `10- Beneath CSE building 1.jpg` and `11- Beneath CSE Building 2.jpg`. The [sign lettering and crest](assets/cse-ground-sign-lettering.webp) and [memorial portrait and inscription](assets/cse-founder-plaque.webp) are perspective-corrected from those photos and sit on modeled tile surrounds. The sign and plaque have separate collision bounds.
- New buildings use `type: "building"` under `buildings`. A duplicate campus building sets `template` to its original model ID and keeps its own dimensions and placement. Changes to the zone model appear on reload in each copy. Trees, prop copies, and exterior doors use typed records under `elements`, alongside transform overrides for existing props. A placed door stores its parent building ID, wall side, and normalized wall offset.
- The `deck` array stores IDs while keeping the object records. Stored entries are detached from the scene and excluded from picking, colliders, and the minimap. Buildings carry their mounted doors, and the pool carries the separately editable portal. Permanently deleted built-in objects use `removedObjects`. Deleting added pieces removes their records. The editor saves the layout against the source map in browser storage.
- The deck captures nearby cards on demand into 2D canvases with one small WebGL renderer. Cached previews follow the stored model's shape and scale. Pointer drags pause captures and use the same snap, land, and wall checks as click placement.
- Deck stacks group related models in the editor. Each placement and deletion still uses a single stored record and the saved deck retains individual IDs. Boundary scaling updates the draft corners uniformly around the bounds center and uses the same apply and undo path as corner edits.
- Undo and redo hold committed transforms, component renaming, placement and deck operations, and land boundary edits in memory. Pool transforms capture the portal, checkpoint, and zone together. Replaying an edit updates the scene and saves the current layout for reload and export. Layout import validates JSON structure from file selection or direct text paste and reloads into the editor.
- Component names can be inspected and edited directly in the layout editor inspector panel (`#editor-object-name`) when an object is selected, and on the placement deck via the card rename button (`✎`, `.editor-card-rename`) or double-clicking the card caption. Renaming synchronizes the in-memory entry, Three.js object hierarchy name, spec label, serialized map records (`map.buildings`, `map.elements`, `map.pool`), and automatically cascades to attached child door labels. Re-sorting, category searching, undo/redo, and JSON export/import fully preserve custom component names.

## Graphics details to preserve

- The application operates permanently in drone flight mode upon entering campus (there is no walking mode). Its altitude spans walking eye height (1.7 m) to 80 m on land, and permits descent down to `-2.55 m` (pool basin floor) when over the campus swimming pool. Left-click or ↑ ascends and right-click or ↓ descends. Horizontal collision reuses the campus footprints with height checks so the drone can clear roofs and blocks descent into them, and confines lateral movement within pool basin walls below ground level. Movement speeds run at 9.0 m/s base and 17.1 m/s boosted sprint (1.9×). The aerial drone audio features a warm, non-fatiguing acoustic profile: low-pass filter cutoff spanning 1,750–3,250 Hz with Q = 0.75 resonance, an aerodynamic prop-wash/motor body triangle oscillator (68–90 Hz, 240 Hz lowpass), a controlled 0.94× base playback rate spooling to ~1.18×, and balanced gain (0.17–0.32). When submerged in the pool or exploring the deep sea, the drone operates as an underwater ROV: the audio engine dynamically engages muffled hydrophone resonance (280–540 Hz lowpass filter with high resonance Q = 1.65, sub-water 52 Hz thruster oscillator, 0.72× base playback rate). Pressing M toggles drone audio mute/unmute with persistent gain ramping. Playback rate, gain, filter cutoff, and pan change smoothly, and the audio fades while exploration is paused.
- DeepSea (`underwater.html`) shares the exact control scheme with campus: WASD (and left/right arrows) for horizontal movement, ↑ or Left-click for ascending, ↓ or Right-click for descending, and Shift for boosted speed (8.5 m/s base, 15.7 m/s boosted swim; 11.0 m/s base, 20.3 m/s boosted climb). The `#world` canvas automatically requests pointer lock on click or pointerdown, ensuring mouse look immediately controls view rotation without requiring manual pause toggles. Neither Space nor C are used for vertical movement, keeping controls completely identical to campus.
- [assets/drone-rotors.wav](assets/drone-rotors.wav) is a 3.55-second mono loop cut from a Phantom 4 Pro hover recording. The source was downmixed, high-pass filtered, normalized, and crossfaded at the loop seam. [UAS Drone Hover 01.wav by DCPoke, CC0](https://freesound.org/people/DCPoke/sounds/387985/)

- Models use `THREE.Group` hierarchies and local transforms. The editor keeps transformable groups separate. The walking view combines repeated geometry into spatial instance batches and rebuilds them after an edit. Fish use animated instance matrices with conservative school bounds. Offscreen kelp, schools, and jellyfish geometry skip animation work.
- Pool wave height adds sine and cosine terms. Kelp uses a travelling sine wave with fixed roots. Fish follow `x = x₀ + R cos(ωt + φ)` and `z = z₀ + R sin(ωt + φ)`, face the tangent, and oscillate their tails. The source comments document the formulas for the graphics report.
- Campus weather preferences are scoped to the browser session. Test with a fresh session or reset the toggles before comparing the default view.

## Browser smoke checklist

Use the static server in the [README](README.md). Inspect the browser console for import, WebGL, or shader errors.

1. Load both entry points. On desktop and a narrow touch viewport, check that the landing art, heading, primary control, and upper-right instrument fit. Enter the scene and pause to check that the canvas remains visible in the compact briefing.
2. On campus, test pointer lock, mouse look, movement, and `Esc`. Hold Shift with WASD or the arrows to boost walking speed, or use Shift+Q and Shift+E to strafe. Plain E opens the editor. Walk through the gate and into a building wall or perimeter wall to check that the camera stays outside the obstacle at `y = 1.7`.
3. Press `1` through `4` and compare the view, active checkpoint, zone label, and coordinates. Cycle `N`, `R`, and `V` and check the visible sky, lights, rain, wet ground, foliage, and fog.
4. From checkpoint 4, enter exploration and walk forward into the pool. Confirm the fade and ocean arrival. Swim through the surface return ring and confirm the campus loads at the pool deck. Try the campus `P` shortcut separately.
5. In the ocean, use `Space`, `Shift`, and `C` to check the depth limits, layer labels, and meter. Look for reef life in the shallow zone, fish and turtles in midwater, and glowing creatures in the abyss.
6. On a touch device or emulated touch viewport, check drag-to-look, directional buttons, ocean rise and sink controls, and pause.
7. Open the editor from the walk view. Pick an object, drag a handle, and edit yaw and size in the inspector. Move a building and a tree independently, then check the minimap, collisions, and the downloaded JSON in a fresh session. Move or rotate the pool and repeat both portal crossings from checkpoint 4.
8. Drop a tree and a building from the palette, then attach a door to the building. Increase and decrease the floor count and change the footprint. Check that the opening dimensions stay fixed and the added door follows its wall after export and reload.
9. Drag a piece onto the deck and check its model preview. Scroll the tray, then drag a card back onto the map with mouse or touch. Test dropping outside the land and over the inspector. Duplicate a building with an attached door and check the copy after a reload. Confirm deletion of a stored piece, then undo it. Clear the map and undo once to restore all the pieces.
10. Load the empty Stamford campus preset. Check that the outline includes the notch and that one undo restores the previous boundary and placed objects. Redo and place a piece from the deck. Draw a concave outline to check custom editing. Check that outside placement is rejected and that walking stops at the outline. Restore the pool and try the portal shortcuts. Export the map and reload it in a fresh browser to check the layout and deck.
